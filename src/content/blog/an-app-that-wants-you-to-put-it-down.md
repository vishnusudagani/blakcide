Almost every app you use is quietly graded on one number: how long it can keep you. Time-on-screen, daily active minutes, sessions per day. It’s the metric the whole industry optimises for — and once you see it, you can’t unsee it in the design of your feeds.

We think it’s backwards. An app that only wins when you *can’t put it down* is, by definition, at odds with the rest of your life.

## The metric we actually care about

The question we hold every feature to isn’t *“did this keep them longer?”* It’s:

> Did your real life get better? Did you walk away more connected to it?

That single reframe changes everything downstream. A feature that keeps you scrolling but emptier fails our test even if it “performs.” A feature that gets you off the app and back to a person passes, even though the dashboard would call it a loss.

## What that looks like in the product

- **A feed built around you, not around engagement.** [Nexus](/nexus/) shows you what actually matters to you — your tribes, your moods, your world — not whatever keeps you agitated and scrolling.
- **Recognition that means something.** Resonance, streaks, and badges reflect real presence and the impact of what you share — not a pile of hollow likes.
- **An AI that nudges you outward.** [Blak](/blak/) reaches out to move your day forward, and hands you to a real human on [Minit](/minit/) when that’s what you need. It’s built to give you back time, not harvest it.
- **No dark patterns.** No manufactured FOMO, no guilt loops, no dependency-by-design.

## Why bother?

Because the attention economy already ran the other experiment, at scale, and we watched how it ended: more connected than ever, and lonelier than ever. We’re not interested in being the most “engaging” app you resent. We’d rather be the one you’re glad you opened and just as glad to close.

Most apps want your attention. Blaksyd wants your life to be better. That’s not a tagline we reach for — it’s the spec.
