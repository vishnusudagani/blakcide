Think about the last time you felt genuinely *heard* — not advised, not fixed, not waited-out while the other person loaded their reply. If you had to think hard, you're not alone. Being truly heard is one of the rarest experiences in modern life, and it’s the entire reason [Minit](/minit/) exists.

The technology was never the hard part. The people are. So here’s what we actually look for.

## A good listener doesn’t fix, rush, or judge

Most of what passes for listening is really just waiting to talk. A good listener does the opposite — they make space. Their job isn’t to solve you; it’s to let you hear yourself think, feel less alone in it, and leave a little lighter.

> You don’t always need an answer. Sometimes you just need a witness.

The listeners on Minit are real people, carefully trained through Blaksyd’s own program and thoroughly screened. We look for people who can:

- listen without judging, rushing, or trying to fix;
- ask the thoughtful question instead of the obvious one;
- sit with a hard feeling instead of rushing to make it comfortable;
- offer real human perspective and encouragement, honestly.

## Safety makes better listeners — on both sides

Here’s something most platforms get wrong: they protect the user and leave the helper exposed. A listener who feels unsafe, cornered, or burnt out can’t show up well for anyone.

So Minit is built with **two-sided safety**. Listeners can exit any conversation immediately, set clear boundaries, take breaks, and lean on supervisor support; we monitor for burnout and we don’t operate under “the user is always right.” Protecting the people who listen is how we keep the listening good.

## Warm, not cold

The worst part of asking for help is starting from scratch — re-explaining yourself to a stranger. On Blaksyd, [Blak](/blak/) hands the listener the *shape* of the conversation first — context, never your private content — so they walk in warm and you don’t have to begin at zero. You stay anonymous by default the whole time.

To be clear: Minit listeners offer real human support, not therapy, and Minit is not an emergency service — in a crisis you’re pointed to the right professionals and helplines, fast.
