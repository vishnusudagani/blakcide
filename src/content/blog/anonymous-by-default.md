People perform when they’re watched and they’re honest when they’re safe. That one fact about human nature shaped one of the first decisions we made about Blaksyd: you are **anonymous by default**, and you reveal yourself only when *you* choose to.

It’s not a privacy checkbox. It’s a precondition for the kind of honesty the whole platform is built to hold.

## Honesty needs safety

The conversations worth having — the ones where you admit you’re struggling, ask the question you’re embarrassed by, or say the thing you’ve never said out loud — only happen when it’s safe to be unguarded. Attach your real name and face, and most people quietly edit themselves. Take that pressure away, and they can finally be real.

So on [Minit](/minit/) and [Nexus](/nexus/), you’re anonymous unless you decide otherwise. A listener doesn’t know your real name or details unless you share them. When you [Reconnect](/minit/) with a listener you trust, you keep your history together — continuity without ever giving up your privacy.

> You shouldn’t have to choose between being known and being honest.

## You control what’s remembered

Anonymity is only half of it. You also control your memory: you decide what [Blak](/blak/) and [Persona](/persona/) carry forward, and you can review, change, or delete it. Blaksyd doesn’t sell your personal data — full stop.

## Anonymous isn’t lawless

Anonymity without safety just becomes a different kind of harm, so we pair it with real guardrails. [Blak](/blak/) is a quiet, default moderator in every Nexus tribe — it keeps the space honest without taking it over — and there’s reporting and escalation when something crosses a line. Two-sided safety protects the people who open up *and* the people who show up to listen.

The goal is simple: a place where you can take the mask off, because for once it’s safe to.
